# The Cabinet — how to use it

A list of what's in your drinks cabinet, on your phone. It shows every open
bottle drawn at the level it's actually at, so you can see what you have
without taking anything off the shelf.

There's no account, no password, and nothing to sign up for.

## Set it up (five minutes, once)

**1. Open this link on your iPhone, in Safari:**

https://rudrodutta11-hash.github.io/inventory/

It has to be Safari. Chrome on an iPhone can't do step 2.

**2. Add it to your home screen.**

Tap the **Share** button at the bottom of the screen — the square with an
arrow coming out of the top. Scroll down the list and tap **Add to Home
Screen**, then tap **Add** in the top right.

You'll now have a Cabinet icon on your home screen, alongside your other apps.

**3. From now on, open it from that icon — not from Safari.**

This matters more than it sounds. Opened from the icon, it runs like a proper
app, works with no signal at all, and your records are safe. Opened from
Safari, iPhone treats it as just another website and can eventually clear it.

## Have a look around first

Tap **Settings** at the bottom, then **Load demo cabinet**. That fills it with
a dozen made-up bottles so you can press everything without worrying.

While the demo is loaded there's a line at the top of the screen reminding you
the bottles aren't real. When you've finished poking about, tap **Clear it** on
that line — then start adding your own.

## The everyday things

**Adding a bottle.** Tap **Add** at the bottom. Type the name. Tap **Save
bottle**. That's genuinely all that's required — the ABV, the age, where you
bought it and the rest are optional, and you can fill them in later or never.

**Recording a pour.** Open the bottle and tap **Pour**, then tap the measure.
The level in the picture drops to match. Two taps.

**Finding a bottle.** Type a bit of its name — or its number — into the
search bar at the top. Every bottle has a number (the big stencilled one on
its picture), so "17" takes you straight to bottle 017.

**Which bottles need drinking.** Anything that's been open a while and is
getting low shows up under "Needs finishing" on the main screen.

## Backing up — the one habit worth having

Your bottles are stored **on the phone itself**. Not on a website, not in an
account. That's why it works on a plane, and it's also the catch: if the phone
goes in the sea and you've never backed up, the list goes with it.

Every so often — after adding a few bottles, say — go to **Settings** and tap
**Export backup**, then **Save to Files** (or email it to yourself). That makes
one file with everything in it: every bottle, every pour, every note and photo.

If you ever get a new phone, install the app there and use **Import backup** to
put it all back.

The app will nudge you with a small reminder after twenty changes. That nudge
is worth listening to.

## What could go wrong, and what to do

**"I cleared Safari's history and my bottles are gone."** If you added the app
to your home screen and opened it from there, clearing Safari doesn't touch it.
If you'd been using it as a website in Safari, clearing website data does
delete it. This is exactly why step 2 above matters. Restore from your most
recent backup.

**"It says a newer backup exists."** That only appears if the optional syncing
was set up. It's asking whether to replace what's on the phone with the copy
stored elsewhere. If you're unsure, tap "Not now" — it never overwrites
anything on its own.

**"I poured the last of a bottle."** It marks it finished and moves it to the
Graveyard, keeping the record. If you have a sealed spare of the same thing it
offers to open that instead.

**"I deleted something by accident."** Deleting asks first and says it can't be
undone. If a bottle is simply empty, use **Mark finished** rather than Delete —
that keeps it in the Graveyard with its notes and score.

## One thing it deliberately doesn't do

It won't look bottles up on the internet or fill in details for you. You type
what you want and leave the rest blank. Most of the time the name is enough.
